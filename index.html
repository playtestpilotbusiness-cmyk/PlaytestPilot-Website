The problem

You know how your game works. New players do not.

After months of development, it becomes hard to see your game like a first-time player. Objectives feel obvious. Mechanics feel natural. UI labels make sense because your team already knows what they mean.

But new players bring no context. They miss prompts, misunderstand goals, overlook key information, and sometimes lose interest before they reach the best parts of your game.

Not knowing where to go

Players wander without understanding the objective or next step.

Missing the objective

Key goals get overlooked entirely, even when displayed on screen.

Missing interactables

Objects and triggers players can interact with go completely unnoticed.

Understanding mechanics too late

Core systems click only after players have already disengaged.

Emberwake Hollow v0.3.2 has a strong core. Combat was the best-rated part of the survey (4.1 out of 5), atmosphere and audio were praised without prompting, and all 6 testers who reached the Boss Zone beat it within the hour.

The problem is getting there. Enchanting, the system the game’s progression is built on, was walked past by 7 of 10 players in the tutorial, and the first equip stalled 8 of 10 for an average of 47 seconds. Every tester who failed to reach the boss had skipped enchanting, and 4 of 10 say they would have stopped before the hour if they had been playing at home.

Positive signal: Combat feel and atmosphere carry the session. “Atmospheric” was the most common single word in open answers, and 5 of the 6 testers who beat the boss reacted positively out loud.

Watch: Level 3 lost 4 players. All four backtracked looking for something they thought they had missed, and all four had skipped enchanting. Part of this may be knock-on from the tutorial, so re-test it after the tutorial changes (see 06).

Highest priority: 7 of 10 players walked past the enchantment station with the prompt on screen. Those who skipped it described Level 1 as hard rather than recognising they were under-powered.

Your questions, answered

Q1

Do players understand enchanting without being told?

Mostly not. 7 of 10 walked past the enchantment station in the tutorial, and “I understood how to make my character stronger” averaged 2.1 out of 5. The 3 who did use it all reached the boss.

Q2

Where do people lose interest in the first hour?

In two places. 2 testers say they would have stopped during Level 1, after struggling without enchantments, and 2 in Level 3, where they backtracked looking for something they had missed. Testers are paid to play the full hour, so this survey answer is the drop-off measure, not session length.

Q3

Does the boss fight land?

Yes. All 6 who reached it won within the hour, 5 reacted positively out loud, and it was the most-mentioned highlight. The one complaint is the audio mix, raised by 4 of the 6.

Reviewer’s note

If you change one thing before Next Fest, make it the tutorial. It is the clearest pattern in this report: the three players who found enchanting all reached the boss, and the seven who didn’t lost time somewhere along the way.

I would hold the Level 3 finding more loosely. Four players is a real pattern, but all four had also skipped enchanting, so part of what looks like a pacing problem may be under-powered players losing confidence. Fix the first twenty minutes, then look at Level 3 again before redesigning it.

The combat is the best thing in this build. Protect it while you change everything around it.

Hugo Mendes · Founder, Playtest Pilot · Reviewed the evidence from all 10 sessions

  • Testers are paid to play the full hour, so drop-off is measured by asking whether they would have stopped at home, not by session length.
  • Patterns seen in 4 or 5 players are worth acting on but are not certainties. Player counts are on every finding so you can judge for yourself.

06Recommendations

Focus areas for the next build

Ordered by player impact. Each one names the problem and the direction. How to solve it, and what it costs to build, is your team’s call: you know the trade-offs, we know what players did.

P1
HighTutorial · Addresses Finding #1

Make enchanting something new players do, not something they pass

The tutorial offers the enchantment station but doesn’t make it matter. A visible prompt alone did not stop 7 of 10 players walking past, and every tester who skipped it lost time later. Direction: players should leave the tutorial having enchanted something and knowing why it made them stronger.

P2
HighUI / UX · Addresses Finding #2

Make the first equip obvious

8 of 10 players hesitated at their first item pickup, for 47 seconds on average. Direction: at that first pickup, players need to see where the item goes and how to put it there, without searching the menus to find out.

P3
MediumLevel design · Addresses Finding #3

Give players a reason to push on through Level 3

4 players backtracked here looking for something they thought they had missed. All four had also skipped enchanting, so re-test this after P1 before redesigning it. Direction: on entering Level 3, players need to know they are on the right path and that something lies ahead.

P4
MediumCombat feel · Addresses Finding #4

Make it unambiguous when an attack lands

6 of 10 players couldn’t tell whether hits were connecting, most of all against faster enemies where animation and movement overlap. Direction: the moment of impact needs to read clearly at any enemy speed. A re-test should check whether the “is this hitting?” comments go away.

P5
LowAudio · Addresses Finding #5

Carry the atmosphere through the boss fight

4 of the 6 who reached the boss found the mix jarring, after praising the audio up to that point. Direction: keep the atmosphere audible under the boss fight’s effects, so a strength doesn’t turn into a complaint at the climax.
