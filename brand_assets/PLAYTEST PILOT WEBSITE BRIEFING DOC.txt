2. BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Playtest Pilot is a done-for-you playtesting service for indie game developers.

It helps indie teams identify where real players get confused, stuck, disengaged, or lose clarity before an important release moment.

Important release moments include:

* Public demo
* Steam Next Fest
* Publisher pitch
* Investor milestone
* Kickstarter demo
* Early access update
* Launch
* First-time player onboarding review
* Milestone build review

Playtest Pilot runs structured playtests with genre-matched PC players. These players record gameplay, complete survey feedback, and provide useful player experience data.

Playtest Pilot then reviews the player behaviour, survey responses, repeated friction points, gameplay recordings, clips, and timestamps before delivering a human-reviewed insight report.

The final report is the product.

The report helps developers understand:

* Where players struggled
* Where players lost clarity
* Where players got stuck
* Where players became disengaged
* What players understood
* What players missed
* Which issues appeared across multiple players
* Which findings are supported by gameplay clips, survey responses, and timestamps
* Which player-facing issues deserve attention before the next milestone

Playtest Pilot is not a QA testing service. Severe bugs may be noted if they affect the session, but the main focus is player experience, onboarding, clarity, pacing, difficulty, engagement, and first-time player behaviour.

Playtest Pilot is not an AI playtesting company. AI may be used internally to help organise evidence, but the public-facing value is real player behaviour, structured analysis, and human-reviewed reporting.

Playtest Pilot is not a SaaS dashboard. The service is done-for-you and report-led.

12. TRUST AND OBJECTION HANDLING

Objection:
Is this QA testing?

Website answer:
No. Playtest Pilot focuses on player experience, onboarding, clarity, pacing, difficulty, friction, and player understanding. Severe bugs may be noted if they affect the session, but bug finding is not the main service.

Objection:
Are testers under NDA?

Website answer:
Yes. Testers sign NDAs before receiving access to unreleased builds.

Objection:
Is my build safe?

Website answer:
Build access, gameplay footage, survey responses, and report materials are handled confidentially. This should be explained clearly on the Service and Book a Playtest pages.

Objection:
What do I actually receive?

Website answer:
The website must show the report clearly. It should show report sections, example findings, survey results, evidence tables, clips, timestamps, and recommended focus areas.

Objection:
Are these random testers?

Website answer:
No. Players are selected based on genre fit, platform suitability, availability, and recording readiness. The website should explain that genre matching improves relevance.

Objection:
Do I get full recordings?

Website answer:
The standard package includes curated clips and timestamps. Full recordings can be made available depending on scope, upload requirements, and file size.

Objection:
Is AI writing the report?

Website answer:
AI may support transcription, clustering, and evidence organisation. Final findings, priorities, and recommendations are reviewed by Playtest Pilot before delivery.

Objection:
What if my build is rough?

Website answer:
The service is designed for playable builds, demos, vertical slices, milestone builds, early access updates, and launch candidates. The build does not need to be finished, but it must be playable enough for the agreed session length.

Objection:
Why not just ask my Discord?

Website answer:
Community feedback can be useful, but community members may already understand the game, may be biased toward the project, or may not behave like fresh players. Playtest Pilot focuses on structured sessions with players selected for genre relevance.

Objection:
Why is this worth €499?

Website answer:
The value is not just collecting opinions. The value is recruiting relevant players, collecting recorded gameplay and survey data, reviewing the evidence, identifying repeated friction, and delivering a clear report that saves the developer time and supports better decisions before a public release moment.
